## 1. Verdict

**Neither family passes the declared rules.** S3 fails only the deflated Sharpe rule (0.58
against 0.80). S3-R1 fails the deflated Sharpe rule (0.59) and the 10% drawdown limit (11.50%).
Under the plan, S3 is not a candidate: it does not go to PAPER as a candidate, the hold-out
(from 2024-10-17) stays locked, and no tuning follows without a new declared plan.

S3 is a clear improvement on S1 in every metric. It is the first strategy here whose Sharpe,
return/drawdown and stability clear their thresholds, and it falls short only on the test that
discounts for the 25 variants tried so far.

## 5. Diagnosis

- **Exposure was the lever.** S3 invests 24–56% (S3-R1 35–88%) against S1's ~20%, and return
  scales with it while drawdowns stay far below B0's.
- **The 2022 bear market cost about 4% (S3) to 7% (S3-R1).** The trend filter cut exposure to
  about half (24–38% in the 2022 folds), but whipsaws around the 150/200-day averages still lost money.
- **The deflated Sharpe is the binding rule.** With 25 trials, a Sharpe of 0.67 over about 4.5
  years is not distinguishable from the best of 25 lucky draws at the 80% level. This is a
  statement about evidence, not proof that S3 has no edge.
- **Risk 1.0% breaks the drawdown limit** (11.5%), so S3-R1 is not usable under the owner's 10%
  limit even if the deflated Sharpe were met.
